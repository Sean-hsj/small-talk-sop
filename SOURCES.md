# Source register and evidence notes

Last checked: **2026-08-18**

This register supports the design and safety boundaries of the SOP. It is not a claim that every example is directly prescribed by a source, nor is it legal advice.

## How sources are used

1. **Law and official policy** define important boundaries, but applicability depends on jurisdiction and facts.
2. **Peer-reviewed and university-hosted research** informs likely benefits, costs, listening practices, psychological safety, and relationship mechanisms.
3. **Professional and language-learning guidance** helps make scripts usable and natural.
4. **Cross-cultural references** supply hypotheses to test locally, never rules for diagnosing an individual.
5. **Team observation and personal preference** override generalized cultural defaults unless doing so would reduce safety or violate policy.

The repository deliberately adopts a preventive standard that may be stricter than a local legal minimum. Follow applicable law, company policy, collective agreements, and professional rules. For a legal determination, use qualified local counsel or the appropriate authority.

## Small talk, listening, and workplace connection

### Workplace small talk research

- [Methot, Gabriel, Downes & Rosado-Solomon, “Office Chitchat as a Social Ritual,” Academy of Management Journal](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2018.1474) — Research behind the “uplifting yet distracting” framing. Supports treating small talk as potentially connective while still reading timing and stopping early.
- [University of Kansas School of Business summary of the office-chitchat study](https://business.ku.edu/news/article/2021/11/11/office-small-talk-proves-more-beneficial-distracting-employees-study-finds) — Accessible explanation of belonging, positive emotion, distraction, reading the room, and natural script endings.
- [Harvard Business Review, “Remote Workers Need Small Talk, Too”](https://hbr.org/2021/03/remote-workers-need-small-talk-too) — Supports intentional, low-pressure social connection in remote work; it does not justify constant interruption or compulsory camera use.
- [Sandstrom & Dunn, “Social Interactions and Well-Being: The Surprising Power of Weak Ties”](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167214529799) — Supports the potential value of brief interactions with acquaintances. It does not show that every person benefits from every interaction.
- [Brooks et al., “It Doesn’t Hurt to Ask: Question-Asking Increases Liking”](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000097) — Supports responsive follow-up questions. This repository adds the one-thread, reciprocity, and stop-signal safeguards to avoid interrogation.
- [Edmondson, “Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams”](https://doi.org/10.2307/2666999) — Supports an environment where people can speak, question, and admit difficulty without interpersonal punishment; used here to strengthen manager responsibility and credible opt-outs.

### Practical conversation and remote-work guidance

- [British Council, “Relationship-building”](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/business/podcasts-professionals/relationship-building) — Greetings, interests, listening, questions, and relationship maintenance for business English.
- [British Council, “Meeting new people”](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/speaking/a1/meeting-new-people) — Introductory language for new colleagues and low-language-level learners.
- [British Council, “Meeting face-to-face”](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/speaking/b1/meeting-face-face) — Natural first-meeting and welcome expressions.
- [University of Minnesota HR, “Asking Powerful Questions”](https://hr.umn.edu/supervising/resources/Asking-Powerful-Questions-0) — Open, closed, leading, and why questions; asking one question at a time and allowing thought.
- [University of Oregon HR, “Remote Work: Communications and Engagement”](https://hr.uoregon.edu/remote-work-communications-and-engagement) — Remote connection, inclusive hybrid meetings, intentional social space, and work-life boundaries.
- [Microsoft 365, “Your Guide to Chat Etiquette in the Workplace”](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/business-insights-ideas/resources/your-guide-to-chat-etiquette-in-the-workplace) — Channel choice, availability, mentions, reactions, and written-chat conventions. Platform details may change and require periodic review.
- [GOV.UK, “Employers and loneliness”](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/employers-and-loneliness/employers-and-loneliness) — Workplace connection, remote work, buddy systems, interest groups, and organizational responsibility.
- [Northeastern University, “Cross-Cultural Communication”](https://graduate.northeastern.edu/knowledge-hub/cross-cultural-communication/) — Curiosity, active listening, self-awareness, and adaptation; explicitly useful as a rejection of one-size-fits-all communication.

## International safety, equality, privacy, and accessibility

### International and multi-jurisdictional anchors

- [International Labour Organization, Violence and Harassment Convention, 2019 (No. 190)](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB:12100:0::NO::P12100_ILO_CODE:C190) — Global reference for violence and harassment in the world of work, including work-related communications, travel, training, events, and third parties. Domestic legal effect depends on ratification and implementation.
- [European Union, “Equal treatment of staff”](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/hiring-managing-staff/employee-rights-representation/equal-treatment/index_en.htm) — EU overview of protected grounds, equal treatment, and harassment in employment.
- [United Nations, Guidelines for Gender-Inclusive Language](https://www.un.org/en/gender-inclusive-language/guidelines.shtml) — Context-sensitive, non-discriminatory language. Pronoun and identity disclosure remain voluntary.
- [World Health Organization, “Alcohol” fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/alcohol) — Health context supporting alcohol-free choices and non-coercive event design. It is not a workplace etiquette code.

### United States

- [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, “Harassment”](https://www.eeoc.gov/harassment) — Protected characteristics, offensive jokes and conduct, severe or pervasive standards, supervisors, coworkers, and third parties.
- [EEOC, “Sex-Based Discrimination”](https://www.eeoc.gov/sex-based-discrimination) — Sex, pregnancy, sexual orientation, transgender status, and sexual harassment.
- [EEOC, “National Origin Discrimination”](https://www.eeoc.gov/national-origin-discrimination) — National origin, ethnicity, and accent-related discrimination and harassment.
- [EEOC, “Religious Discrimination”](https://www.eeoc.gov/religious-discrimination) — Religious practice, dress, harassment, and accommodation.
- [EEOC, “Disability Discrimination”](https://www.eeoc.gov/disability-discrimination) — Disability discrimination and accommodation entry point.
- [EEOC, “Age Discrimination”](https://www.eeoc.gov/age-discrimination) — U.S. age-discrimination overview; supports avoiding age-based assumptions about ability, opportunity, and retirement.
- [U.S. Supreme Court, Bostock v. Clayton County](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/17-1618_hfci.pdf) — U.S. Title VII decision concerning sexual orientation and transgender status.
- [U.S. National Labor Relations Board, “Your Right to Discuss Wages”](https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/your-rights/your-rights-to-discuss-wages) — Important safeguard against misusing etiquette rules to suppress protected discussion of pay, hours, working conditions, or concerted activity.

### United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia

- [Acas, “Discrimination and the law”](https://www.acas.org.uk/discrimination-and-the-law) — UK overview of protected characteristics, discrimination, harassment, and employer responsibilities.
- [Acas, “Neurodiversity at work”](https://www.acas.org.uk/neurodiversity-at-work) — Individual differences, communication, masking, sensory needs, and avoiding assumptions.
- [Acas, “Age discrimination”](https://www.acas.org.uk/age-discrimination) — UK workplace age-discrimination overview and examples across recruitment, training, pay, promotion, and dismissal.
- [UK Information Commissioner’s Office, “Special category data”](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-is-special-category-data/) — Health, race, political opinion, religion, sex life, and sexual orientation as high-risk data categories under UK GDPR.
- [UK Health and Safety Executive, “Managing drug and alcohol misuse at work”](https://www.hse.gov.uk/alcoholdrugs/develop-policy.htm) — Workplace policy and support context for alcohol risk.
- [Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety, “Bullying in the Workplace”](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/psychosocial/bullying.html) — Gossip, isolation, offensive jokes, privacy intrusion, and responses to uncomfortable conduct.
- [Australian Human Rights Commission, “Work-related sexual harassment: Know your rights”](https://humanrights.gov.au/know-your-rights/rights-of-individuals/workplace-rights/positive-duty-sex-discrimination-act/work-related-sexual-harassment-know-your-rights) — Work-related settings beyond the physical office, repeated invitations, sexual comments, bodies, social media, events, and travel.
- [Government of Canada, “Work place harassment and violence prevention”](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/workplace-health-safety/harassment-violence-prevention.html) — Federal workplace prevention framework and resources; jurisdiction and coverage vary.
- [WorkSafe New Zealand, “Bullying”](https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/topic-and-industry/bullying/) — New Zealand workplace bullying prevention and response resources.

### Disability and media accessibility

- [U.S. CDC, “Communicating With and About People with Disabilities”](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-and-health/articles-documents/communicating-with-and-about-people-with-disabilities.html) — Respectful communication, person preference, and avoiding dehumanizing stereotypes.

## China-specific law, workplace relationships, and cultural context

### Law and privacy

- [最高人民法院：《中华人民共和国民法典》全文](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html) — 第一千零一十条涉及违背他人意愿的性骚扰和单位义务；第一千零三十二条起涉及隐私权和个人信息保护。适用和救济请由合格专业人士结合事实判断。
- [国家互联网信息办公室：《中华人民共和国个人信息保护法》](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2021-08/20/c_1631050028355286.htm) — 个人信息处理的合法、正当、必要和最小范围原则，以及敏感个人信息。用于支持“不截图、不转述、不建立隐私社交档案”的预防性规则。
- [国家法律法规数据库](https://flk.npc.gov.cn/) — 核对现行法律文本的官方入口。重大法律更新或具体条款引用应在发布前重新核验。

### Relationship and face research

- [Chen, Chen & Huang, “Chinese Guanxi: An Integrative Review and New Directions for Future Research”](https://doi.org/10.1111/more.12010) — 综合大量关系研究，说明 guanxi 是复杂、多层的机制；不支持把关系简化成送礼、套话、喝酒或人情债。
- [Hwang, “Face and Favor: The Chinese Power Game”](https://doi.org/10.1086/228588) — 关于面子、人情和互动机制的经典分析。用于解释体面退出和公开逼问的风险，不用于预测每个中国人的人格。
- [Cultural Atlas, “Chinese Communication”](https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/chinese-culture/chinese-culture-communication) — 含蓄表达、停顿、和谐和面子的跨文化概览。只作为待验证的观察假设。
- [Cultural Atlas, “Chinese Business Culture”](https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/chinese-culture/chinese-culture-business-culture) — 关系、层级和商务互动概览；部分内容偏传统或涉外商务，不能直接等同于现代内部职场。
- [Cultural Atlas, “Chinese Naming”](https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/chinese-culture/chinese-culture-naming) — 中文姓名和称呼背景；本人选择和实际团队惯例优先。

## Limitations and maintenance

- Country sections are adaptation prompts, not population descriptions.
- Legal pages summarize general rules and may change. The project does not determine whether conduct is unlawful in a particular case.
- English-speaking Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, continental Europe, and China all contain major regional, organizational, professional, class, generation, and individual differences.
- Research on “small talk” often measures group averages and does not justify forcing interaction on an individual.
- Product conventions for Slack, Teams, WeChat, Feishu, DingTalk, video platforms, and social media change faster than core communication principles.

Recheck legal and platform sources at least annually and whenever material policy or legal changes occur. When a source conflicts with current official guidance, update or remove it and record the decision in the release review.
