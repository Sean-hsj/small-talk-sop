# Cross-cultural design guide / 跨文化设计说明

This guide explains why the English and Chinese SOPs deliberately diverge and how to adapt either one without stereotyping.

本文解释中英文 SOP 为什么有意不完全对应，以及如何在不制造刻板印象的前提下做本地化。

## Use the smallest reliable culture unit

Before applying a country-level pattern, check smaller and more relevant units in this order:

1. The individual’s stated preference
2. The relationship and its history
3. The immediate team’s observed norm
4. The company, profession, and industry
5. The city or region
6. The country or language environment

National patterns can generate a hypothesis, never a verdict. Observe, ask, mirror lightly, and update.

## Shared safety, different defaults

| Dimension | International / multinational English default | Mainland China, Chinese-language default | Adaptation rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Greeting | First name is common in many organizations; confirm pronunciation and local title norms | Organization-specific titles and role labels are more visible; first name may feel close or foreign-company-like | Ask or mirror how colleagues address the person; never invent intimacy |
| Purpose | Often a brief bridge before work; explicit friendliness may be expected | Often combines warmth, relationship maintenance, and situational care | Keep it light until the other person expands |
| Direct exit | “I’ll let you get back to it” is usually natural | “你先忙”“回头聊” often protects both sides’ face | Offer the other person the socially easy exit |
| Personal topics | Family, age, health, relationship status, salary, and identity usually need stronger permission | Some teams ask these more readily, but familiarity does not erase privacy or power pressure | Treat normalized questions as higher-risk, not automatically acceptable |
| Hierarchy | Informal language can coexist with strong organizational power | Titles, seating, meal rituals, group chat, and who speaks first can make hierarchy more visible | The higher-power person carries responsibility for making interaction optional |
| Disagreement | Some teams value explicit preferences; understatement and humor vary by region | Indirect signals and face-saving may carry more meaning in some teams | Hear hesitation and non-expansion as data; do not force a direct “no” |
| Meals and alcohol | Social events may include alcohol but participation must remain optional | Shared meals can be a stronger relationship ritual; toasting or drinking pressure may occur | Never equate alcohol, late hours, or attendance with loyalty |
| Digital chat | Async messages, status indicators, threads, and emoji often carry social cues | Work chat may blend operational and relational talk; group visibility and after-hours boundaries matter | Match channel, urgency, group size, and privacy |
| Banter | Can signal closeness in some UK, Irish, Australian, or team contexts | Teasing and nicknames also occur, but public loss of face can be costly | Do not start with teasing; only reciprocate established, non-identity-based humor |

These are design defaults, not descriptions of every person. A Beijing tech startup, a state-owned enterprise, a Shenzhen factory, a Shanghai professional-services firm, and a multinational R&D team can differ sharply. So can London finance, a US nonprofit, a German lab, and a remote global startup.

## The adaptation loop

Use **Notice → Ask → Try → Calibrate**:

1. **Notice:** How do people greet, address, pause, change topics, and leave?
2. **Ask:** When the choice matters, ask a low-cost preference: “Do you prefer Alex or Dr. Chen?” / “我怎么称呼您比较合适？”
3. **Try:** Use one low-risk behavior, not a full imitation of the group.
4. **Calibrate:** If the other person expands and reciprocates, continue one step. If not, reduce intensity or exit.

Mirror formality and pace, but do not mirror gossip, discriminatory humor, intrusive questions, alcohol pressure, or after-hours boundary violations.

## Culture-switching examples

### A senior colleague appears informal

- English default: “Hi Priya—do you prefer Priya or Dr. Nair?”
- 中文默认：“您好，我是新来的陈嘉。请问怎么称呼您？”若已观察到团队用法：“我听大家叫您王工，我也这样称呼您可以吗？”
- Why: observed informality is not permission to guess a preferred title.

### Someone gives an ambiguous short answer

- English: “Got it—I’ll let you get back to your morning.”
- 中文：“好，那你先忙，我就不打扰了。”
- Why: neither person has to issue a face-threatening rejection.

### A team uses teasing as bonding

Newcomers and leaders should not initiate it. First observe whether everyone, especially the least powerful person, can opt out and tease back without consequence. Never use identity, body, accent, family, romantic life, health, religion, or performance as material.

## When local custom and safety conflict

Choose safety. “That is how people socialize here” does not justify harassment, discrimination, coercion, retaliation, outing someone, sharing health information, forcing alcohol, or making access to work depend on social participation.

Use the company’s reporting and support channels for serious or repeated conduct. This SOP can help with ordinary awkwardness; it is not a private resolution requirement for misconduct.
