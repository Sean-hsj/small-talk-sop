# v1.2.0 manager communication-style add-on review

Review date: **2026-08-18**

Scope: optional English and Chinese guidance for adapting to a manager's observable communication preferences.

This is an editorial self-audit, not independent certification or proof that a script will work with every manager.

## 1. Publication question

The requested add-on was allowed only if a responsible classification and review loop could pass the existing SOP standard.

**Decision:** publish a limited communication-preference add-on. Reject fixed personality typing.

The accepted model changes delivery format. It does not change facts, ethics, consent, privacy, disagreement, work access, or reporting rights.

## 2. Model-selection review

### Rejected approaches

- MBTI, Enneagram, astrology, attachment, clinical, and other fixed labels
- “Male/female manager,” age, nationality, race, disability, or generation scripts
- Assigning a permanent type from one meeting, mood, title, or second-language behavior
- Advice based on flattery, mirroring mannerisms, masking, appeasement, or hidden influence

These approaches fail because they invite overconfidence, stereotypes, privacy problems, or manipulation and do not describe the task-specific choice a reader must make.

### Accepted approach

Six provisional dimensions passed initial review:

1. fast ↔ deliberate pace;
2. explicit ↔ indirect communication;
3. task-first ↔ relationship-first focus;
4. big-picture ↔ detail level;
5. experiment ↔ risk-control decision style;
6. expansive ↔ reserved social bandwidth.

They are observable, dimensional, context-dependent, and revisable. They are not measures of competence, character, empathy, or leadership quality.

The mandatory loop is Observe → Hypothesize → Ask → Implement → Recalibrate. High-impact assumptions cannot bypass a direct preference question.

## 4. Bilingual design review

The English and Chinese add-ons share the same safety architecture but are not line-by-line translations.

English examples use common multinational patterns such as headline-first updates, pre-reads, one-to-one agendas, pilots, and explicit owner/checkpoint confirmation.

Chinese examples account for “拍板,” “留痕,” “汇报,” “午餐会,” indirect refusal, title hierarchy, and the risk of treating social adaptability as “会不会来事.”

Neither guide describes all English-speaking or Chinese workplaces. Actual person, task, team, and policy remain primary.

## 5. Role and context coverage

The add-on is written for an employee communicating with:

- a direct manager;
- manager +1 or +2;
- a deputy, assistant, dotted-line, or matrix manager;
- several managers in a group setting.

Existing power rules still apply. A senior person's preference never overrides the employee's privacy, safety, work access, or right to raise necessary disagreement.

Covered contexts include coffee, kitchen, fixed one-to-one, weekend return, leave return, one-to-one lunch, two- or three-person interaction, group lunch, lunch meeting, feedback, disagreement, deadlines, and second-language ambiguity.

Gender, age, marriage, children, pregnancy, disability, culture, and other identity or life-stage information are excluded from style inference.

## 9. Evidence review

The add-on uses existing research on psychological safety, question design, cross-cultural humility, accessibility, equality, and harassment for its safety architecture.

Two workplace personality meta-analyses were checked through canonical DOI metadata. They show dimensional, probabilistic research relationships, not a basis for assigning a manager a scriptable type.

The release therefore does not map Big Five scores to phrases and does not claim that its six communication dimensions are a validated personality instrument.

Publisher pages may reject automated requests even when the DOI resolves. Source maintenance must distinguish access restrictions from invalid citations and still perform periodic human review.

The 2026-08-18 automated sweep covered all 47 registered URLs. Thirty-two returned HTTP 200, fourteen returned HTTP 403 from publisher or protected sites, and the NLRB page did not complete within 20 seconds.

The two new DOI records resolved to publisher pages and their titles, authors, and publication years matched Crossref metadata. Restricted or timed-out results are not treated as proof of failure or proof of current content.

## 11. Limitations and monitoring

- Preference questions may be answered vaguely or may change with context.
- Employees may lack enough access or safety to ask directly; the neutral default remains concise, truthful, and work-focused.
- “Observable” behavior can still be misread. The loop reduces overconfidence but cannot eliminate it.
- The examples have editorial and adversarial review, not controlled outcome testing across industries and regions.
- Local policy, collective agreements, law, role duties, and accessibility needs can require a different format.

Monitor issues for stereotype drift, scripts used for compliance or masking, unequal access, and cases where a manager's style changes by audience or identity.

## 12. Final decision

**Pass v1.2.0 for inclusion, subject to the repository's local and remote documentation checks.**

The add-on is approved because it gives usable adaptation without fixed personality claims and because all 14 adversarial tests and 10 critical gates pass.

If a future change adds diagnosis, identity inference, appeasement, truth suppression, unequal opportunity, or an abuse-as-style exception, withdraw the add-on until the failure is fixed and the full gate set is rerun.
