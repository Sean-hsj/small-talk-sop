# English workplace small talk — one-minute card

Use in international and multinational workplaces. Individual and team preferences beat country stereotypes.

## SAFE

| Step | Do | Say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scan** | Check availability, privacy, power, and channel | “Morning” may be enough |
| **Ask lightly** | Start from shared context; one easy question | “How’s your week going?” |
| **Follow** | Acknowledge → one follow-up → share a little | “That sounds fun. What was the best part? I’ve been meaning to try it.” |
| **Exit cleanly** | End on warmth; do not restart | “I’ll let you get back to it—good luck with the launch.” |

## Continue or leave?

**Continue:** detail + return question + new thread + available pace.

**Leave:** repeated short answers + no return question + attention returns to task/door + busy status or delay.

One short answer may be style. Two low-engagement signals mean lower intensity or exit. Never make someone refuse twice.

## Topic ladder

### Usually low pressure

- Shared surroundings, meeting, event, public commute or weather
- Work-adjacent interests, tools, learning, public projects
- Food options, books, shows, podcasts, public events
- Broad weekend/rest plans with an opt-out
- Specific appreciation of an action or skill

### Follow only if they open it

- Family, partner, hometown, culture, housing, money
- Health, leave, grief, diet, fitness, religion
- Dating, salary, career move, identity, politics

### Do not initiate as small talk

- Pregnancy, fertility, age, diagnosis, medication, disability reason
- “Real” origin, immigration status, gender history, sexual orientation
- Body, attractiveness, weight, clothing fit, romantic availability
- Politics/religion in a polarizing or power-imbalanced setting
- Gossip, confidential work, identity jokes, reasons for declining food/alcohol/events

No topic is automatically safe. Follow the person, not the category.

## Phrase bank

### Open

- “Morning—how’s it going?”
- “I don’t think we’ve met. I’m Alex from Operations.”
- “That was an interesting session. What did you think?”
- “How are you finding the new role?”
- “Anything planned for the weekend?”

### Keep it going

- “That sounds like a lot.”
- “What part did you enjoy most?”
- “I’ve been meaning to try that.”
- “How about you?”
- “Just to make sure I understood…”

### Exit

- “I’ll let you get back to it.”
- “Anyway, I won’t keep you.”
- “I need to jump onto another call. Catch you later.”
- “Good talking with you—have a good one.”

### Set a boundary

- “I keep that part of my life private.”
- “I’d rather not discuss that at work.”
- “Let’s talk about something else.”

### Repair

- “Sorry—that was too personal. You don’t need to answer.”
- “That comment wasn’t appropriate. I’m sorry.”
- “Sorry, Mei.” Then correct and continue.

### Remote

- “Hi Maya—how was the conference? No rush.”
- Put the work purpose in the first message; do not send only “Hi” and wait.
- Respect status, time zone, channel, and a reaction-only response.
- Get separate permission before taking, recording, posting, forwarding, or tagging workplace photos or video. Attendance is not consent to publication.

### Plain English exits

- “I need to get back to work. See you later.”
- “I need to join another call. Talk to you later.”
- “It was nice talking to you.”

### Touch and invitations

- Start with words or a small wave when unsure. Do not initiate a hug or touch a person or assistive equipment without permission.
- A personal invitation gets one attempt. A decline, silence, subject change, or vague delay closes it; do not try another channel.

## Manager rule

More power means lighter topics and clearer exits. Never link social disclosure, camera use, event attendance, alcohol, or chat activity to work opportunity.

## Emergency brake

If repeated conduct is sexual, discriminatory, coercive, retaliatory, or threatening, it is not awkward small talk. Use company reporting, safety, union/employee-representative, or appropriate external channels. This card is not legal or crisis advice.
